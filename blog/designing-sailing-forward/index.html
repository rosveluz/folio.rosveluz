A Publication for an Established Organization

From June 2013 to March 2023, I worked on the design and production of Sailing Forward, AMOSUP's quarterly publication. My responsibilities included page layout, covers, image selection, infographics, and preparing files for print.

AMOSUP's history reaches back to 1960, when its two predecessor unions were formed. They merged under the AMOSUP name the following decade. That history belongs to the organization; my work on the publication was one part of its ongoing communications. Read AMOSUP's official history.

The publication brought together union news, seafarer stories, education, welfare information, and organizational updates. It needed to accommodate different kinds of material within a recognizable editorial format.

The Working Brief

An issue typically ran to 32 pages or more, with a turnaround of roughly two weeks. Articles and photographs came through the editor-in-chief and the President, who also reviewed and approved the finished layouts.

My role covered the visual work from the cover through the interior pages to print preparation. The recurring constraint was straightforward: lengthy articles had to fit within fixed page allocations.

That made the available space part of the design brief. A page could not be considered only as an individual composition; it also had to carry its assigned content within the issue.

Lengthy Articles, Fixed Space

Fitting copy into an allocation is not simply a matter of making the type smaller. Text length, column structure, headline size, and image placement all affect the space a story occupies and how comfortably it can be read.

The selected spreads show column-based layouts alongside larger headlines and supporting photographs. Those elements give an article structure while working within the boundaries of the page.

For this kind of publication, restraint has a practical purpose. The design needs to leave enough room for the information while giving readers clear entry points into longer stories.

Covers and Visual Explanation

The covers show a consistent masthead across issues, with photography and cover lines changing to reflect each edition's subject. That combination provides continuity without requiring every cover to use the same composition.

Inside the publication, the material sometimes called for a different treatment. A shore-leave report, for example, paired its article with an infographic spread presenting survey figures and comparisons.

The infographic offered another way into the information alongside the longer text. This is an example of the range within the assignment: editorial layout, image selection, and visual explanation were parts of the same publication.

Through to Print

The work also included preparing the files for print. The finished layout was therefore a production deliverable, with review and approval forming part of the path to completion.

A roughly two-week turnaround meant the assignment combined design decisions with a defined production window. The recurring page-allocation challenge remained relevant throughout: each issue needed to bring its content together within an agreed format.

What This Work Represents

Looking across these editions, the work represents sustained involvement in an established organization's communications. It required a range of design skills, but those skills served a shared purpose: presenting varied editorial material in a consistent publication.

That is the kind of work I want to continue doing for businesses, trade unions, and organizations. Websites, publications, and supporting brand materials can each contribute to a clear and considered public presence.
